### 1. The operational flow, end to end

The warehouse's job is to take inventory in, hold it accurately, and get it back out again, fully traceable. Every stage below both does something physical and updates a specific set of system objects.

**Inbound / Receiving.** A truck is expected because of an **Inbound Order** (header + detail lines: a SKU and expected quantity per line) — created manually, via ERP integration (**SCExpert Connect**), or absent entirely (**Blind Receiving**, where the warehouse has no advance notice and builds the record as goods are unloaded). The Inbound Order is exported into a **Receipt**, the document that records what actually shows up (it can differ from what was ordered — partial shipments, over-receipt). A single Inbound Order can spawn several Receipts (one truck at a time).

Within a Receipt, physically unloading and identifying goods creates **Loads** (see §2). Loads can come from manual entry at a workstation, from an **SCExpertMobile** (RF/handheld) scan-driven flow, from importing an **ASN** (Advanced Shipping Notice, which carries pre-declared pallet/handling-unit contents so a single scan receives everything on it), or from Blind Receiving. Each Load is stamped with an **Inventory Status** (Available, Quality Hold, Inspection Hold, Damaged, Prelabel, …) reflecting its condition at receipt. When every SKU on a Receipt has at least one Load, the Receipt is "Fully Received"; it is then **Closed**, which stops further receiving against it and notifies the host ERP.

**Putaway.** Loads sitting in the receiving lane get moved to a proper storage location. The operator scans the Load; the system evaluates the applicable **Putaway Policy** (selected via a **Putaway Policy Matcher**, see §3) to pick a destination: try the SKU's preferred location, then its preferred storage region (**Put Region**), then the best-scoring location warehouse-wide. If the assigned equipment can't physically reach that destination, the system inserts an intermediate **Handoff Location** and generates a follow-up task. Putaway can happen at Load granularity, Handling-Unit granularity (whole pallet/tote moved as one), or Handling-Unit-Load granularity (loads on a mixed HU are dispatched to different final locations one at a time).

**Storage / Inventory Control.** Once put away, a Load simply sits in a **Location** until something needs it. Ongoing housekeeping: **Inventory Moves** (relocate a whole Load), **Inventory Adjustments** (add/subtract quantity, split a Load, change its SKU or UOM, create a Load without a Receipt — each requires a Reason Code), **Inventory Holds** (change status, e.g. to quarantine inventory), and **Limbo** handling (see Counting below). **Cross Docking** is the exception to "storage happens": a **Flow Through** immediately allocates a fresh receipt to an outbound order and ships it with minimal dwell time (still counted as Four-Wall inventory while on site); a **Transshipment** goes further and never enters Four-Wall inventory at all — the system tracks only the pre-packed Handling Unit passing through.

**Replenishment.** **Pick Locations** (storage locations dedicated to one SKU, for fast picking) run low and must be refilled from bulk storage. Four flavors: **Normal** (scheduled, off-peak, tops up to a Max level once a Min threshold is breached), **Hot** (triggered live during wave planning when a pick location is over-allocated relative to what's physically there — urgent), **Opportunity** (no threshold logic; seizes a chance to top up a location when suitable inventory happens to pass by, avoiding a storage-then-replenish round trip), and **Manual** (operator-initiated, fills to max). Replenishment can move a **Full** Load, a **Partial** quantity picked off a Load, or go **Negative** (pull the whole Load to the pick face and let picking consume directly from it, later putting away or opportunity-replenishing the remainder). **Zone Replenishment** does the same at the level of a **Put Region** instead of a single location — useful when you'd rather manage aggregate stock in an area than per-slot thresholds.

**Picking.** Triggered by **Outbound Order Planning**: the system matches each order line to a **Picking Policy** (via the **Picking Policy Matcher**), tries to allocate available inventory (choosing a specific Load and/or Location), and if successful generates a **Pick List** — one or more **Pick Details**, each representing units to move from a Location/Load to a destination. Orders can first be grouped into **Waves** (batches processed together, e.g. all frozen orders on one schedule) and/or assigned to **Shipments** (truckload groupings) and/or pre-assigned to **Staging Lanes** before planning — each optional and policy-driven.

Picking **types**: Full Pick (whole Load), Partial Pick (quantity off a Load into a Handling Unit), Negative Pallet Pick (take the whole Load, return the unallocated remainder to storage), Parallel Pick (one picker fills several orders' Handling Units simultaneously), Z Picking (an alternate, reachable location substitutes for a location that can't physically be picked from directly, e.g. high shelving). Separately, **picking methods** decide how order lines are grouped into a Pick List before any of the above types execute — Pick by Order, by Customer, by Ship To, by Item, by Truck, by Route, by Wave, or Single Picking. If inventory can't be allocated, the shortfall becomes an **Order/Wave Exception**, which may auto-trigger Hot Replenishment or require manual resolution (cancel the exception, short-pick and cancel, or short-pick and leave open for a later attempt).

**Packing, Consolidation, Staging.** Picked units land in a **Handling Unit** (a physical container: pallet, tote, carton — tracked with its own ID). **Consolidation** lets staged Handling Units be merged, split, or freshly created before shipment to optimize the outbound load. **Packing** (mainly a parcel/pick-and-pack concern) confirms an order has been boxed — packing without using Consolidation stops the system tracking individual Loads inside the package, so the order can't be staged/loaded until that's resolved. **Staging** moves completed Handling Units to the **Staging Lane** the order is assigned to, ready for loading; when the last Load of an order reaches staging, the order status becomes STAGED.

**Loading & Shipping.** Once a vehicle is at the dock, Handling Units/Loads are loaded onto it — verified against a **loading sequence** if one is defined, and against the correct **Shipment**. First Load loaded → order status LOADING; last Load → LOADED, and those Loads become **Shipment Loads**. **Shipping** (per order, or per Shipment to close out every order in it at once) is the point of no return: it decrements Four-Wall inventory, notifies the host ERP, and generates shipping documents; shipment/order data is frozen afterward. Loads can be pulled back off a truck before shipping via **Unloading**.

**Returns.** Returns arrive through the same Inbound path as any other receipt — an Inbound Order of type "return" (or Blind Receiving if unannounced) — and get whatever Inventory Status reflects their condition (e.g. Return, Damaged, Inspection Hold) so they're kept apart from sellable stock until processed. There is no separate "returns module": it's the receiving flow with a different order type and status.

**Counting / Cycle Counting.** Three granularities: **Load Counting** (verify one Load's quantity/location), **Location Counting** (count everything sitting in one Location — every Load present must be accounted for, or the uncounted remainder is pushed to Limbo), and **Location Bulk Counting** (count a SKU's total quantity in a Location without resolving it to individual Loads; a discrepancy here kicks off a full Location Count to find the culprit Load). **Cycle Counting** schedules these across the warehouse in rolling batches (by warehouse area, over N days) instead of one disruptive full physical inventory. A **Count Book** scans all locations of a given count type and generates one Count Task per location; **Recounting Discrepancies** re-targets just the locations that didn't reconcile.

A discrepancy that can't be reconciled becomes a **Limbo** Load — inventory the system knows exists somewhere but can't currently place correctly. Limbo Loads of the same SKU can be merged; resolving/adjusting a Limbo Load notifies the host of the quantity change. **Inventory Adjustments are distinct from counting** — adjustments are direct corrections and do *not* generate Limbo Loads; counting discrepancies do.

**Work Center (light manufacturing).** Kitting, assembly/disassembly, and value-added services (labeling, inspection, cleaning) run through **Work Orders**, always Made-to-Stock — even a Work Order triggered by an Outbound Order first produces inventory, which is then allocated normally. Assembly/Disassembly Work Orders consume a **Bill of Materials (BOM)**: component SKUs and quantities per unit of the parent SKU, definable at the SKU master-data level and importable ("Populate BOM") onto a specific order.

### 2. Core entity glossary

**Client** — the owner of the inventory. "All inventory, orders, SKUs and workflows are defined under a specific client." In a 3PL warehouse each customer is a separate Client; a single-owner (non-3PL) warehouse may still split into Clients per internal division. A SKU is only unique in combination with its Client — two Clients can use the same SKU code for different products.

**Company** — any external business entity a Client transacts with: a vendor (source of inbound goods) or a customer (destination of outbound goods, or source of a return). One Client can work with many Companies.

**Contact** — address/phone/name details tied to a Client or a Company ("a telephone book"). A Contact belongs to exactly one Company; if two Clients work with what is conceptually the same real-world contact, it must be created twice (once per Client).

**SKU** (Stock Keeping Unit) — "the minimal referenced container used to stock inventory in the warehouse." Identified uniquely by SKU code + Client. Carries a **Lowest UOM** (the smallest unit inventory is ever tracked or returned in) and a hierarchy of higher **UOMs** (case, pallet, …) each with its own barcode, weight/dimensions and units-per-lower-UOM conversion. New SKUs are flagged `New` and cannot be received until master data (especially UOMs) is verified — receiving is often the first real test of SKU setup. SKUs also carry **Velocity** (A/B/C, how fast it moves — feeds Putaway scoring), **SKU Class** (drives which Load/Load-Detail attributes like batch, expiry, serial must be captured — see below), **SKU Group** (a looser categorization used in policy matching and reporting), and Hazard / Storage / Transportation **Class** codes used the same way.

**SKU Class** — a template attached to one or more SKUs that defines *what extra data must be captured* about them: SKU Attributes (item-level, e.g. shelf-life days), Load Attributes (per-receipt, e.g. batch/expiry — dairy is the docs' canonical example), and Load Detail Attributes (per unit/case within a Load, e.g. serials). A SKU has exactly one Class; a Class can cover many SKUs.

**Load** — "a homogeneous physical unit of inventory that can be moved and handled as a single unit." One Load = one SKU (never mixed), though a shipment of one SKU can spread across several Loads. Every Load has a unique **Load ID**, a UOM, a quantity, an **Inventory Status**, and a current **Location**. This is the atomic inventory record the system reasons about for allocation, picking, putaway, counting, and billing.

**Handling Unit (HU)** — a physical container that groups Loads for movement/tracking: pallet, tote, carton, plastic box, cage. Has its own unique ID and can be identified either by that ID or by the Load ID of anything on it. A **Handling Unit Type** defines physical/capacity characteristics (cube, weight capacity, dimensions). This product's "Handling Unit" plays the role often called an LPN (License Plate Number) in other WMS products — the docs never use the term "LPN."

**Location** — a unique identifier for a physical storage/processing spot. Carries operational attributes: **Pick Region** (grouping used for pick-list sequencing and replenishment targeting), **Put Region** (grouping used for putaway/zone-replenishment targeting), **Warehouse Area** (used for task-assignment/access rules), Aisle/Bay/Level, Usage Type (picking, storage, handoff, …), Pick Type, Storage Type, Material-Handling (MH) Type (which equipment can reach it), capacity/dimensions, and Access Type (FIFO/LIFO). Distances between Locations are computed from a **Warehouse Map** of X-Y-Z **Nodes** joined by directional **Edges** (aisle or cross-aisle), which also encodes one-way/blocked-aisle restrictions.

**Pick Location** — a Location dedicated to exactly one SKU (unique key: Location + SKU) so it can be replenished and picked predictably. Has its own Normal/Hot Min/Max thresholds independent of the general Replenishment Policy defaults.

**Zone / Put Region / Pick Region** — three names for warehouse groupings used for three different purposes: Put Region for putaway/zone-replenishment targeting, Pick Region for pick-list sequencing and (separately) as the *source* region in zone replenishment. A "Zone" in the Zone Replenishment feature specifically means a Put Region.

**Inventory Status** — the condition of a Load's inventory: Available, Quality Hold, Inspection Hold, Damaged, Prelabel, Return, Display, Expired, Trash, etc. Distinct from **Activity Status** (see Task below), which describes what process the Load is currently *in* (Picking, Putaway Pending, Replenishment Pending, Staged, Loaded, …) rather than its physical condition.

**Load Attributes** — batch/lot, serial, expiry date, manufacture date, size, weight, style, color, harvest, kosher flag, opened-container flag — captured per Load (or even per unit/case, as Load Detail Attributes) when the SKU's Class requires it. These are the fields that make picking/putaway policies distinguish "identical SKU, different lot" scenarios.

**Inbound Order** — expected incoming inventory: header (Client, order type — damage/purchase-order/return, source Company, expected date) + detail lines (SKU, expected quantity). Not itself a physical record — it authorizes and forecasts a Receipt.

**Receipt** — the document for one actual truck/shipment's arrival, created by exporting an Inbound Order, manually against a Bill of Lading, via ASN, or via Blind Receiving. Tracks quantity received vs. expected; status progresses New → Receiving → (Fully Received) → Closed, or Canceled if nothing was ever received.

**Outbound Order** — the document driving shipment of inventory out: header (Client, order type — customer/direct/vendor, target Company, priority, staging lane) + detail lines (SKU, quantity, inventory status required). Status progresses through New → (Wave/Shipment Assigned) → Planned → Picking → Picked → Staged/Packed → Loading → Loaded → Shipped, with Cancelled/exception branches. "Order" without qualifier in this domain almost always means Outbound Order.

**Pick List** — "a set of picking instructions that specifies the products and storage locations required to fulfill one or more orders... a logical unit of work assigned to a warehouse operator." Produced by planning; may span multiple orders/customers depending on the active Picking Method. Contains one or more **Pick Details** (the actual From-Load/From-Location → To-Load/To-Location instructions and their statuses).

**Wave** — a batch of Outbound Orders grouped for combined planning/picking control (e.g. "all frozen orders scheduled together"). Created manually or by a Wave Assignment Policy. Planning a Wave triggers allocation, applies Picking Policies, and produces Pick Lists/Tasks for the whole batch at once.

**Shipment** — groups Outbound Orders that leave the warehouse together, "typically represents a truck load," tied to a dock **Door**, carrier, vehicle, driver, seal numbers. Distinct from an individual Order: shipping a Shipment completes every Order assigned to it.

**Staging Lane** — the physical location an order's picked Handling Units are consolidated into before loading, assigned manually, via Staging Lane Planning, or automatically via a Staging Lane Assignment Policy.

**Work Center / Work Order / BOM** — see §1. A Work Order is always for a single SKU (the one being assembled, disassembled, or serviced); its BOM lists component SKUs and required quantities.

**Task** — the unit of work dispatched to an **SCExpertMobile** operator (Pick, Putaway, Replenishment, Count, Yard Move, …), carrying From/To Load/Location/Container, priority, assignment method (manual vs. the Task Manager auto-assigning on "Get Task"), and standard-vs-actual execution time for labor performance tracking. Tasks are handed out according to the active **Task Assignment Policy**, filtered by the operator's Role, Handling-Equipment capability, and current Warehouse Area/location.

**Handling Equipment** — the physical tool an operator uses (forklift, pallet jack, reach truck, …), each with a type defining weight capacity, speed thresholds, and which Location MH Types it can access. Logging into SCExpertMobile with an equipment ID pulls in its default printer, terminal type, and access rights automatically.

**Appointment / Dock / Yard / Carrier** — belong to the Yard/Receiving edge of the flow: an **Appointment** schedules a vehicle's arrival to a **Door** (a dock Location) at a time slot; the **Yard** is the outside-the-building area where trailers/vehicles wait, tracked via **Yard Locations**, **Yard Equipment**, and Gate Check-in/Check-out events; a **Carrier** is the transport company delivering or collecting the shipment (a Company acting in that role, referenced on Receipts/Shipments/Appointments).

**LPN / License Plate Number** — not used by this product; the closest equivalent concept is the **Handling Unit** (container-level tracking ID) or, for a single unit of inventory, the **Load ID**. Do not assume "LPN" is a synonym engineers will find in code/UI — it isn't a term this product uses.

### 3. Policies, plugins, and system settings

**"Policy" in this product means a rule set that changes runtime behavior without a code change.** Nearly every non-trivial decision the system makes — where to put a Load away, which Load to pick from, when to replenish, which task a picker gets next, which wave/shipment/staging-lane an order lands in — is delegated to a named Policy record, and *which* Policy applies to a given transaction is itself decided by a **Policy Matcher**: an ordered list of condition rows (AND logic within a row; first full match wins, by ascending priority number) keyed off things like SKU, SKU Group/Class, Client, order type, company, carrier, wave mode, etc. If no policy matches, the operation (e.g. putaway) cannot complete — "at least one policy must be defined," even if it's a catch-all with no conditions.

The policy families that recur across the docs:
- **Putaway Policy** (+ Matcher) — eligible Put Regions/Storage Types, same-SKU-vs-empty-location rules, and a **Scoring** table (aisle, bay, height, distance-to-pick-location, cubic size, …) used to rank candidate locations once eligibility is established. Values can be negative to invert a scoring dimension; zero makes it irrelevant.
- **Picking Policy** (+ Matcher) — Pick Method (by Order/Customer/Item/…), allocation rules (full-allocation-only vs. partial), a **Break Strategy** (when to split one Pick List into several, by cube/weight/item-count/HU capacity), a **Release Strategy** (auto-release, label printing triggers, short-pick handling), and its own **Scoring** table for choosing which Load to pick from.
- **Replenishment Policy** — From-Pick-Region, replenishment type (Full/Partial/Negative), and Scoring for choosing which Load to draw from.
- **Task Assignment Policy** (+ Task Assignment table linking Roles to policies) — ordered list of task types/subtypes a given Role should be offered, plus Scoring (distance, creation date, task priority).
- **Wave / Shipment / Staging-Lane Assignment Policies** — criteria for auto-grouping Outbound Orders (route, volume, door range, carrier, max orders, …) so waves/shipments/lanes fill themselves on a schedule instead of by hand.
- **Routing Policy / Routing Plan Strategy** (RoutingExpert) — distance, time, vehicle-allocation and break rules for the route optimizer; Strategy picks which Policy applies per order/client/vehicle-type, same matcher pattern as everywhere else.

**Plugins** live under **SCExpert Connect**, the integration layer to host (ERP/e-commerce) systems. A plugin is one data flow in one direction (import or export) for one Business Object (SKU, Client, Inbound Order, …), implemented against a transport (file drop, HTTP listener, API, or a named connector like Shopify/Magento/WooCommerce/BCD365/SAP-B1). **Import** plugins run continuously as a background service polling active plugins; **Export** plugins are event-triggered (an `EVENTREGISTRATION` fires when e.g. an order ships, and the matching plugin sends the data). Field mapping between SCExpert's schema and the host's is defined per plugin as an **XSLT** transform, editable visually (a canvas with source/target nodes and draggable connections, plus `For Each`/`If` logic blocks and reusable variables) or as raw XSLT text. **Transaction Keys** tell an import plugin which incoming field(s) identify an existing record (update) vs. a new one (insert).

**System Parameters** are a flat global config table (`Setup > System > System Parameters`) covering things with no natural policy home: session timeouts, password rules, date/time formats, default pick method, licensing, default skin, and various delivery-app toggles (e.g. `AllowDepartWithPartialLoad`). The **Code Table** is the drop-down-value registry (e.g. `OUTORDTYPE`, `INVADJRC`) — adding a new outbound order type or adjustment reason code is a Code Table edit, not a schema change.

**The practical consequence for engineers**: the *same deployed code* can behave completely differently for two customers because Putaway/Picking/Replenishment/Task-Assignment policy rows, System Parameters, and Code Table entries are all per-tenant data, not code. A ticket that says "putaway sent the load to the wrong location" is very rarely a code bug — it is almost always "which policy matched, and why" (check the Matcher's priority order and conditions first).

### 4. The satellite products

**YardExpert** — manages everything outside the four walls: **Yard Locations** (doors/gates a trailer can occupy), **Yard Equipment** (tractors/trailers/vehicles physically in the yard), **Yard Movements** (relocate equipment within the yard, executable from the workstation or via an SCExpertMobile "Yard Jockey" task), and **Gate Check-in/Check-out** against a scheduled **Appointment**. It hands off to WarehouseExpert at the dock door — an Appointment can auto-create a **Dock Schedule**, and receiving/shipping continue in WarehouseExpert once the trailer is docked.

**RoutingExpert** — plans optimized multi-stop delivery/pickup routes. Central object is the **Routing Set**: a bucket of Outbound/Inbound/cross-dock "requirements" for one distribution date, planned into **Routes** (each with Depot, Vehicle, Driver, Territory, Start/End points, cost, distance/time totals) using a genetic-algorithm optimizer governed by **Routing Policy** (distance/time/vehicle-allocation/break rules) and **Routing Plan Strategy** (which policy applies to which order). It connects to WarehouseExpert by consuming the same Outbound/Inbound Order objects — when integrated, an Outbound Order gains Route/Routing-Set/Stop-Number fields and extra statuses (Wave Assigned, Ship Assigned, …) it wouldn't have standalone.

**DeliveryExpert** — executes the last mile once a route is planned: managerial route/task management on the desktop (assign Vehicle+Driver, Load/Depart/Return/Close a route, manage **Route Tasks**/**Packages**/**Route Items**), paired with the **SCExpertDriver** mobile app drivers use to scan loading, navigate stops, confirm deliveries/pickups (signature, photo, confirmation-number, or scanned-document), and report exceptions. Route Tasks are Delivery or Pickup against a Client/Document (Inbound Order, Outbound Order, Flow Through, Transshipment, or a standalone Route Task). DeliveryExpert is the execution/tracking layer that sits downstream of RoutingExpert's planning.

**BillingExpert** — turns warehouse activity into invoiced charges. An **Agreement** (per Client, with a recurring Period and optional Min/Max charge caps) contains **Agreement Lines**, each scoped to one transaction type (Inbound, Outbound, Storage, Assembly, Disassembly, Value Added, Constant) with its own **Billing Basis**, **Price List** (tiered pricing — Linear/Step/Total calculation modes), and optional **Price Equation** for compound formulas. Running an Agreement on its schedule produces **Charges** (and Charge Lines) from eligible WarehouseExpert transactions in the period; storage billing in particular reads Load receive/ship dates to prorate across periods. **Additional Charges** exist for one-off billing outside any Agreement. This product is entirely downstream of WarehouseExpert's transaction history — it doesn't drive warehouse behavior, only meters it.

**SCExpertMobile** (formerly "RDT") — is not a separate business-logic product but the handheld/RF execution surface for WarehouseExpert (and Yard) tasks: receiving, putaway, picking, replenishment, counting, inventory-control moves, yard moves. It runs the same Task Manager / Policy-driven flows described in §1 and §3, just from a scan-first UI instead of the desktop workstation. "RDT" as a term for this device/app survives throughout the docs and codebase even though the product is now branded SCExpertMobile — expect both names in tickets and code. See the product-config-model reference §6 for how RDT NG screens are actually configured/authored.

### 5. Vocabulary traps

- **"Order" is ambiguous by itself.** Inbound Order (expected receipt) and Outbound Order (expected shipment) are entirely different objects with different statuses and different downstream documents (Receipt vs. Pick List/Shipment). Unqualified "order" in warehouse-activity docs usually means Outbound Order; in RoutingExpert/DeliveryExpert it can mean either.

- **Load vs. Handling Unit vs. LPN.** A **Load** is one SKU's inventory quantity (the atomic record); a **Handling Unit** is the physical container multiple Loads can ride on (possibly mixed SKUs). The product does not use the term "LPN" anywhere — don't expect it in code or the UI. A Handling Unit "loses ID" (its contained Loads' individual identities merge) only in locations explicitly flagged `Lose ID` — elsewhere Loads on an HU stay individually addressable.

- **"Status" is at least two different fields.** **Inventory Status** (Available, Damaged, Quality Hold, …) describes physical condition of the goods. **Activity Status** (Picking, Putaway Pending, Replenishment Pending, Staged, Loaded, …) describes what process currently has the Load. A Load can be Available *and* Picking simultaneously — these are orthogonal axes, not one status field.

- **Shipment ≠ Order, and Shipment ≠ Route.** A **Shipment** (WarehouseExpert) groups Outbound Orders leaving together, tied to a dock door — think "one truckload." A **Route** (RoutingExpert) is a planned multi-stop itinerary for a vehicle/driver, tied to Territory/Depot — think "one driver's day." An order can be Shipment-assigned without ever being Route-planned (pure WarehouseExpert use) and vice versa in some integration configurations.

- **Wave ≠ Batch ≠ Pick List.** A **Wave** groups Outbound Orders before planning. Planning produces one or more **Pick Lists**, each of which may itself bundle multiple orders depending on the **Picking Method** (Pick by Order gives one order per list; Pick by Customer/Item/Truck/Route/Wave deliberately mix orders into one list). "Batch picking" in general WMS parlance maps loosely onto Wave + a non-single Picking Method here, not to one specific named feature.

- **Plan Order vs. Soft Plan Order.** *Soft Plan* is read-only simulation — no allocation, no Pick Lists, safe to run repeatedly. *Plan Order* actually reserves inventory and creates picking work. Confusing the two in a ticket repro ("I planned it and nothing got picked") usually means Soft Plan was used.

- **"Policy" is configuration data, not a coded rule.** See §3 — resist the instinct to grep for business logic when a ticket describes wrong putaway/picking/replenishment behavior; check the customer's Policy and Policy Matcher rows first.

- **Putaway Policy Matcher vs. Putaway Policy** — the *Matcher* only decides *which* Policy record applies (a selection step); the *Policy* itself then decides *where* the Load goes (eligibility + scoring). The same Matcher-then-Policy split repeats for Picking, and loosely for Task Assignment (Task Assignment ties Roles to a Policy; the Policy's own Details define what tasks that Role receives, in what order).

- **Full / Partial / Negative recur as a type triad** across Picking (Full Pick / Partial Pick / Negative Pallet Pick) and Replenishment (Full / Partial / Negative Replenishment) with parallel meanings each time: Full moves an entire Load, Partial moves a sub-quantity, Negative moves the entire Load to the point of use and pushes the leftover back afterward.

- **Cross Docking has two distinct sub-flows that are easy to conflate.** **Flow Through** goods still count as Four-Wall inventory and follow normal move/consolidate/load/ship steps, just with (near-)zero dwell time. **Transshipment** goods never become Four-Wall inventory at all — only the Handling Unit is tracked, not its contents as discrete Loads.

- **"RDT" and "SCExpertMobile" are the same product**, old name and current name respectively — both appear in the docs, expect both in code/tickets. RDT devices/timeouts also appear as a distinct concept from Workstation (desktop) sessions in System Parameters (`RDTLOGOFFTIME` vs. `CRTLOGOFFTIME`) — they're configured and time out independently.

- **Client (WMS sense) vs. "client" (generic software sense).** In this product a **Client** is always the inventory-owning tenant/customer entity, never a client application, browser, or API caller. Don't let the generic-engineering meaning bleed into WMS domain discussions.

- **Company vs. Client vs. Contact — a strict containment, not synonyms.** Client owns inventory. Company is who the Client ships to/receives from. Contact is a specific address/person under a Company (or under a Client itself, or under DeliveryExpert's Depot/Site/Driver). A Company can have many Contacts; a Contact belongs to exactly one Company (or one Client if unattached to a Company).

- **UOM conversions are always expressed relative to the SKU's Lowest UOM**, and *all* system-level inventory balances are held in that Lowest UOM — a "1000 units" figure in an inventory view is 1000 lowest-UOM units, not 1000 of whatever UOM was last displayed. Watch for this when reading quantity fields in code or reports: a raw quantity column is UOM-less without also knowing the SKU's Lowest UOM.

- **Counting vs. Adjustment — different consequences on discrepancy.** A **Count** that finds a mismatch creates a **Limbo** Load pending Admin review; an **Inventory Adjustment** is a direct, immediate correction and never produces Limbo. If a ticket mentions "Limbo," the entry point was counting, not an adjustment screen.
